Halloween in New Mark

Saturday, October 31 · Get the candy bowl ready.

What to expect

When will the doorbell ring?

Trick-or-treating in New Mark usually begins around 6:00 p.m. on Halloween night. Some homes have seen well over 100 trick-or-treaters, while homes tucked farther back may receive fewer. Your immediate neighbors can offer a helpful guide to how busy your block tends to be.

Turn on your porch light if you are participating. If you are sitting Halloween out or have finished handing out candy, turn your porch light off.

Lots of trick-or-treaters. Please slow down.

Our streets fill with trick-or-treaters on Halloween night. Please park in your driveway where possible, keep walkways clear, and drive slowly. Watch carefully for people walking in the roadway and crossing between parked cars.

Halloween lovers of ALL ages trick-or-treat in New Mark! Some houses even have special treats just for the adults. Help keep the evening fun and welcoming for everyone.

Campbell Drive speed humps

Just seven more signatures.

After nearly two years of neighbors working together, the Campbell Drive speed-hump petition is close to its signature goal. According to organizer Robyn Dunlap’s latest update, 82 of the 118 affected households have signed in support. That puts us just seven short of the 89-signature goal needed to meet the City’s 75% support threshold for funding consideration.

Last weekend, neighborhood volunteers went door to door and collected five additional signatures, bringing the petition closer to its goal.

For Robyn and other supporters, the effort is about making everyday trips through the neighborhood safer. Without sidewalks along Campbell Drive, neighbors share the roadway with vehicles as they walk to bus stops, the pool, and the clubhouse. Speeding has also raised concerns for wildlife near the pond.

During the first week of October, volunteers will deliver letters with a petition page to the doors of households that have not yet responded. If you support the proposal, please sign and return your page to Robyn Dunlap at 9617 N Campbell Drive, or call or text her at 816-835-6739 to arrange pickup.

One signature per household: Only one member of the household may sign, and that person must be listed on the property deed.

If you do not support the speed humps, simply send Robyn a text message at 816-835-6739 and your household will be removed from the follow-up list.

Reaching the signature goal would move the request forward for funding consideration; it does not mean installation has been approved or scheduled. Whether your answer is yes or no, responding will help wrap up this neighborhood effort.

Leaf cleanup is coming

Keep our streets, storm drains, and pond clear.

North-area city collection

Monday, October 26

For New Mark’s Monday trash route, set out leaf and brush bags for October 26. The city’s North-area collection week runs October 26 through 30, with pickup on your regular trash day.

Please keep leaves and grass clippings out of the street. Debris can block storm drains and wash into the neighborhood pond.

Need help raking? See our resident vendors for neighborhood lawn and leaf services.

City schedule and collection rules
Before you set it out

Bag it, bundle it, keep it clear.

  • Use paper lawn bags; no plastic bags.
  • Limit: 20 bags and bundles total per household, each weighing no more than 40 pounds.
  • Tie branches with twine or jute rope. Bundles may be up to 4 feet long and 2 feet across; branches may be no thicker than 3 inches.
  • Set out after 3 p.m. the day before pickup and by 7 a.m. on collection day.
  • Keep bags and bundles at least 4 feet from trash and recycling carts.

Proposed covenant amendment · November annual meeting

Building reserves for future improvements

At the annual meeting in November, homeowners will vote on a proposed covenant amendment to establish a transfer fee on each home sale in the neighborhood. The funds would go into a Capital Improvement Account to help build reserves for large neighborhood projects.

How would it work? Under the proposal, the transfer fee would be a one-time charge to the new homebuyer at closing, appearing as a debit on the buyer’s closing statement. It would apply when a home is sold.

Setting money aside for capital improvements would help the association prepare for major projects and the long-term needs of our shared spaces.

This is a proposal, not an approved fee. Please plan to participate in the November annual meeting to learn more, ask questions, and vote on the amendment.
